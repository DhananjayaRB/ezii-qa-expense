Core Concept: The Petty Cash Box
Think of a physical metal box in an office that holds a small amount of cash for day-to-day expenses (coffee, stationery, cab fares, etc.). This software digitizes that process. A "cashbox" here is a digital entity representing a fund managed by a specific person (the Cashier). 

Petty Cash Management System - Core Business Logic
1. Primary Business Goal:
To digitally manage one or more petty cash funds, replacing a physical cash box. The system must provide a complete, accurate, and immutable audit trail for every rupee in and out.

2. Core Functional Entities:

Cashbox: A digital fund with a current balance.

Attributes: Name, Description, Designated Cashier, Current Balance, Status (Active/Inactive).

Cashier: An employee user authorized to manage a specific cashbox.

Transaction: Any event that changes the cashbox balance. All transactions must be recorded in a Ledger.

Ledger Entry: A record in the audit trail. This is the most critical concept.

3. The Ledger: The Heart of the System

Purpose: To ensure the current balance of any cashbox can be proven and traced back to its origin. It prevents errors and fraud.

How it Works: The ledger is a log of daily balances. Each entry is a snapshot of a single day for a single cashbox.

Key Fields in a Ledger Entry:

Date

Opening Balance (Balance at the start of the day)

Debit (Total money added to the box that day)

Credit (Total money removed from the box that day)

Closing Balance (Balance at the end of the day)

Formula: Closing Balance = (Opening Balance + Debit) - Credit

4. Fundamental Workflows & Rules:

A. Starting a New Cashbox:

An admin creates a new cashbox, names it, assigns a cashier, and sets an initial amount (e.g., ₹10,000).

Business Rule: This action must create the first ledger entry.

Opening Balance = 0

Debit = ₹10,000 (the fund is established)

Credit = 0

Closing Balance = ₹10,000

B. Performing a Transaction (e.g., Cash Payment):

A user records a payment of ₹500 for office supplies from the "Main Office" cashbox.

The system must:

Find today's ledger entry for the "Main Office" cashbox.

If it exists, add ₹500 to the Credit column and re-calculate the Closing Balance.

If it doesn't exist, create a new entry for today. The Opening Balance will be yesterday's Closing Balance. Then add the ₹500 Credit.

The Golden Rule: The Current Balance of the cashbox is always equal to the Closing Balance of its most recent ledger entry.

C. The Non-Negotiable Rule: Cascading Balance Update

Problem: What if you need to correct a transaction from yesterday?

Logic: Changing yesterday's balance breaks the chain. Yesterday's closing balance is today's opening balance.

Solution: After updating any ledger entry, the system must automatically recalculate all subsequent entries for that cashbox.

Update Entry for Day 2 -> Recalculate its Closing Balance.

Day 3's Opening Balance is now wrong. So, recalculate Day 3's Closing Balance.

Repeat this process for Day 4, Day 5, etc., all the way to the present day.

Why: This guarantees that the entire history is mathematically consistent and the current balance is always correct.

5. Key Transaction Types:

Cash Receipt: Money added to the box (e.g., an employee repays an IOU, reimbursement from the bank). Increases Balance. -> Debit

Cash Payment: Money taken from the box (e.g., buying supplies, giving an advance). Decreases Balance. -> Credit

Fund Transfer (Contra): Moving money between two cashboxes or between a cashbox and a bank account.

From Cashbox A's perspective: It's a Credit (money leaving).

From Cashbox B's perspective: It's a Debit (money arriving).

IOU (I Owe You): An employee takes cash from the box (a Credit), creating a liability. When they repay it (a Debit), the liability is settled.
